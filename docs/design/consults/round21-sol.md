**A. Shape:** Yes—the proposal is the right shape: one typed proposal mechanism, one approval dialog, and one immutable audit record. Keep proposals concrete and executable, scoped to one session, with an explicit before/after diff and reason. Reuse the same mechanism for existing stop menus. Remove only the notion that every framework alternative must be represented as a proposal: routine choices such as retry or cancel can remain direct dialog actions, while any action that changes the declared plan, bypasses a gate, or changes the outcome requires approval and a reason. In terminals, require an explicit confirmation rather than merely printing lines.

**B. Verified steps:** Yes. Approval should invalidate the affected step and everything downstream whose correctness depends on it, then rerun execution, checks, and cross-provider review. Do not automatically reopen unrelated verified work. The approval dialog should state exactly which completed steps and validations will be invalidated before the person approves.

**C. Blocking:** Block the affected session’s progression, not the whole solution loop. Independent sessions and steps may continue. Unattended runs should continue wherever dependencies permit, but must never infer human approval or silently choose an override. If the proposal blocks the critical path, record the run as `awaiting-operator` and exit or suspend cleanly rather than occupying a worker indefinitely. The AI may autonomously choose ordinary, policy-compliant recovery moves—retry, revise unfinished work, or cancel—but not approve its own exception.

**D. Who originates a hold:** Let the AI propose `close as held`; require human approval. Preventing it from proposing does not remove the escape hatch—it merely encourages guessing nonexistent flags or editing configuration outside the record. The real risk is that models will suggest holding too quickly whenever publishing becomes difficult. Control that through presentation and policy: label it as an override, show the unmet promise and attempted remedies, require a specific reason, and place retry/fix alternatives ahead of hold. Approval, not proposal authorship, is the trust boundary.

**E. Rejection risk:** Approval fatigue is the largest risk. If developers see frequent modals, vague AI prose, or dialogs for harmless replanning, they will approve reflexively or disable the framework. Show a modal only when human authority is genuinely required: changing already accepted scope, invalidating verified work, bypassing a verdict or gate, or changing the promised outcome. Make it one screen with the exact diff, consequences, reason, and three clear actions; remember nothing and never bundle unrelated changes.

**One recommendation:** Implement a single typed `proposal` record and approval flow, but distinguish ordinary recovery actions from authority-requiring changes. Every proposal should contain an executable operation, target session, before/after state, reason, invalidated validations, and proposed outcome; approval should apply that operation atomically and record proposer, approver, time, and result. Make `awaiting-operator` a first-class resumable state so the mechanism supports both interactive and unattended runs without weakening truthful closure.
