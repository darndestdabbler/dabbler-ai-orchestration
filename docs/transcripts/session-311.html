Session 311 of 316: A person may authorise going past a stop, and the record says so

Scope: packages/router/src/session.ts and cli/session.ts (the verb), packages/router/src/drive.ts (going on past each kind), packages/router/src/driver.ts (the ways on), packages/router/src/gates.ts, packages/router/src/progress.ts (the projection), packages/router/src/transcript.ts, packages/router/src/cli/consult.ts (UNATTENDED_DENIED), the schema the new rows need, the tests named after each, docs/driving-a-session.md

Why. The operator, 2026-11-11: "developers will abandon this framework, and then all the work that has been done on it and all the rule integrity will not have mattered at all, if the framework rules prevent human operators from getting a session over the finish line."

There is no way today for a person to say: I have read this gate, it is wrong here, let this session past it and write down that I said so. The ways on are to satisfy the rule, to move it where a verb exists for that (plan amend, verify reopen), or to give the work up. session close --force is the one bypass, it passes bookkeeping gates and never evidence, and it marks every open session in the plan complete. The Mechanic cannot help: its brief forbids it to weaken a gate or write the record, so when the wall is a rule it has nothing it may do -- mechanic-log.md has no entry and no run on this machine carries a Mechanic event. In a consumer repository the framework is an installed package, so a wrong rule is a wall until the next release.

Round 23 (docs/design/consults/round23-synthesis.md) put the operator's proposal to Sol and Argon. Both drew the same line, and it is the rule here: a rule may be waived and a record may never be falsified. The record keeps what actually happened -- the test failed, no verdict came, the check refused -- and adds that a person authorised going on, and why. It never says the gate was met.

What. One person's verb, dabbler session waive --reason "<why>", that takes the session past the stop it stands at, once, on the record.

Step 1 -- the verb and the row. session waive is a person's, refused to an engine in isAPersonsVerb's words: report the step blocked and say what should be waived and why. It needs a standing stop of a kind step 2 names and a reason. It writes one row to the run's own exceptions.jsonl -- the stop's kind, code, step and reason as the framework wrote them, the person's reason, who and when as the record already names them, and the commit and tree it was given at -- and a line on the activity log. A waiver is of THAT stop: the next stop, or the same rule refusing a tree that has since moved, needs its own.

Step 2 -- going on. session next after a waiver carries on past the stop instead of into it, and what was unmet stays written as unmet:

  • rejected-thrice on a step's checks: the step is accepted with each check's real result on its row.
  • tests: the run of record stays a failed run, and the session goes on to the land.
  • verification, every code of it: the verdict stays what it was -- none, or the findings as they stand -- and the session goes on to the run of record.
  • close: the gates that refused are recorded as waived, and the close completes this session and no other.

A close gate that reads evidence a waiver already covered reports waived, naming the row, rather than refusing the same fact a second time: one decision by the person, not one per gate that reads it.

Step 3 -- what the record says. The session closes complete, and its verdict is whatever it truly was; no new status word. The projection dabbler status prints carries the session's exceptions, the close's own line says "closed with N authorised exception(s)", and the transcript page shows each one where it happened -- what was unmet, who authorised going on, and their reason -- in the page's ordinary voice, not as a failure.

Step 4 -- the ways on, and who may not. Each stop step 2 names offers the waiver among its ways on, with a cost that says what is let through and what the record will say. close --force's help says a single gate is passed with session waive and that --force abandons a plan. UNATTENDED_DENIED gains "waive a stop", and the Mechanic's brief says the waiver is the person's to type. docs/driving-a-session.md gains the rule -- waive, never falsify -- and the verb.

Non-goals. A stop with nothing to go past: engine, crash, interrupted, budget, funds, tree, land, blocked, and rejected-thrice where no work plan was written -- each has its own way on, and a waiver cannot make a push or a provider answer. publish, and a release session altogether: an unpublished release is not closed as one. Any change to a verdict, a round or a test run already written. A standing or plan-wide waiver, or a setting that waives without the person. An engine or a Mechanic waiving. Removing close --force. The request, the independent check and the Work Explorer, which are session 312's.

Tests. In drive.test.ts: a waived tests stop goes on to the land with the failed run still failed; a waived verification stop goes on with the verdict unchanged; a waiver does not carry to the next stop. In gates.test.ts: a gate over waived evidence reports waived and does not refuse. In progress.test.ts and transcript.test.ts: a session closed past a waiver carries the exception.
