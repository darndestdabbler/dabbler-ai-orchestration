# Consult round 19 — gemini-3.1-pro-preview, direct API (26 s)

VERDICT: adopt with changes
1. Choosing between two concrete resolutions is better because it gives the author a clear, actionable path forward instead of a vague mandate. The failure mode of simple UPHOLD/OVERRULE is the author guessing wrong on the fix, leading to a rejected final round. The failure mode of the two-resolution choice is that both proposed resolutions might be structurally flawed or break the build, forcing the Auxiliary to pick a "lesser evil" that still fails.
2. Call the Auxiliary as soon as the primary UPHOLDS a disputed finding (typically round 2 or 3). A dispute followed by an uphold represents a hard deadlock between the two models. Waiting until round 7 wastes API costs, inflates the context window, and delays resolution. Early arbitration directly serves the operator's budget and patience constraints.
3. Stick to a strict two-way choice. Giving the Auxiliary a third "write-in" option violates the "fewer moving parts" constraint and turns the tie-breaker into a blind architect. A newly invented resolution from the Auxiliary would lack prior scrutiny and could introduce new errors. Final-offer arbitration cleanly bounds the problem and forces the Primary and Author to propose their most defensible resolutions.
4. The primary should conduct exactly one final review round strictly scoped to verifying the fix. To prevent reopening the argument, the framework must prompt the primary to evaluate *only* whether the diff faithfully implements the Auxiliary's chosen resolution, returning a simple PASS or FAIL without raising new findings. Tests must also pass. If tests fail or the primary fails the implementation, the session halts for human review to prevent a new loop.
5. The primary should state its alternative resolution *only* when it upholds a disputed finding. Most findings are simply accepted and fixed by the author in the first round. Forcing the primary to write a concrete, detailed resolution for every routine finding wastes tokens, increases cost, and slows down the standard review cycle.
6. Ship early arbitration with a strict binary choice. When the primary upholds a dispute, it must append concrete resolution steps. The Auxiliary is immediately called to pick either the author's stance or the primary's resolution. The author implements the winner, tests run, and the primary gets one final boolean (PASS/FAIL) review scoped entirely to execution fidelity.
BIGGEST RISK: The Auxiliary chooses a resolution that sounds logically sound in prose but breaks the build or fails tests when actually implemented, leaving the author trapped in the final verification step.
