- **Issue 1: The race-vs-contention verdict is unsupported and contradicts the required treatment of failures persisting under isolation**
  - **Category:** Correctness
  - **Severity:** Major
  - **Failure scenario:** Session 2 relies on this verdict, excludes `vsix-first-run-walkthrough` from its three-green control, and adopts eight workers while the full-isolation, parallel-only Mode 1 failure remains unresolved. This is probable rather than hypothetical: the evidence records one failure in only three fully isolated eight-worker runs, and both Mode 1 occurrences happened under parallel load. Developers then continue receiving materially unreliable Layer 3 results at the adopted setting.
  - **Acceptance criterion:** `JUDGMENT - Does the revised evidence classify the two failure modes separately, record the full-isolation eight-worker Mode 1 failure as the required product-race candidate unless controlled repetitions substantiate another cause, and remove every causal exclusion not supported by the samples?`
  - **Details:**
    - **Violation:** Step 4 requires that failures persisting under full isolation be recorded as “**product races**” and “**written down rather than relabelled as flakiness**.” Section 3 instead concludes that the test is “flaky on its own terms” and asserts “NOT CPU contention,” “NOT shared state,” and “NOT parallelism at all.”
    - **Impact:** The central race-vs-contention deliverable is presented as settled when it is not, undermining the validity of Session 2’s worker-selection control and merge decision.
    - **Evidence:** The document records two distinct modes but reasons across them as though they were one population. Mode 1 occurred twice at eight workers, including once among the three fully isolated runs. Mode 2 occurred alone and therefore says nothing about whether Mode 1 is schedule-sensitive. Likewise, zero failures in three twelve-worker runs does not refute contention: the sample is too small, and a higher worker count does not guarantee that this particular test encountered a strictly heavier concurrent schedule. The claim that Mode 2 is network-bound is also not established merely because progress is gated on a real `pip install`; the evidence contains no pip result proving that network delay, rather than extension progress or refresh behavior, caused the timeout.
    - **Location:** `docs/session-sets/117-bounded-test-parallelism/s1-worker-sweep-DENICI.txt`, section 3; corresponding conclusions in `decisions.jsonl` and `s1-conventions.md`.
    - **Fix:** Separate the modes, report only observed facts, preserve Mode 1 as the spec-required race finding unless additional controlled evidence distinguishes it, and avoid estimating a worker-independent failure rate from heterogeneous runs.

  - **Category:** Completeness
  - **Severity:** Major

  - **Details:**

    - **Location:** `docs/session-sets/117-bounded-test-parallelism/s1-worker-sweep-DENICI.txt`, sections 1–2 and “Not measured”; `ai-assignment.md`; first entry in `decisions.jsonl`.
