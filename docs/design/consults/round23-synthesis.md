# Consult round 23 — synthesis

2026-11-11. One brief, `round23-brief.md`: an exception process, so a
person can always get a session over the finish line, and why the Mechanic
has fixed nothing. Answers: `round23-sol.md` (`gpt-6.1-sol`, direct API)
and `round23-gemini.md` (`gemini-4-argon`, direct API). Claims were checked

## What was put to them

The operator's proposal: the Mechanic writes a script and names the rules
to set aside; a reviewer guards the rules and must name a within-rules
path or assume the Mechanic right; the Mechanic may dispute; the Auxiliary
resolves; failing that all three state their positions; the person
authorises; the framework runs the script and logs each exception as
authorised by the human. Separately, a way to have any block checked for
whether it is fair.

## Where both agreed

- **Waive, never falsify.** A person may authorise going past any gate.
  The record keeps the real result and adds the authorisation; it never
  says the gate was met.
- **Named one-shot waivers the router applies and records**, a person's
  verb, with the Mechanic's ordinary edits beside them. Not a script the
  framework executes: a script can write the record, and policing one is a
  guard for a guard.
- **One independent check, as advice.** It never gates the person, and an
  unreachable adviser never refuses the waiver. A within-rules path that
  is named is tried rather than argued. Dispute and the Auxiliary on
  request only.
- **The adviser reads the record itself**, never the Mechanic's summary,
  and the person is shown a card: the rule's own words, what is let
  through, the alternative, and what the record will say.
- **Reuse `dabbler triage`** as the fair-block check rather than build a
  second classifier.
- **A neutral record**: closed with authorised exceptions, visible and not
  a failure.

## Where they split, and the call

- **The Mechanic.** Argon: delete it and `mechanic-log.md`, and make
  triage the one diagnostic. Sol: keep it and have its proposals use
  waiver requests. Sol's is taken. The operator's ruling of 2026-09-22
  stands -- a fresh instance working in prose is the way past a wall --
  and what it lacked was something it may ask for. Its log entry becomes
  the request.
- **"If the reviewer cannot name a path it must assume the Mechanic is
  right."** Sol pushed back: finding no alternative is not agreement.
  Taken: the adviser says "none found" and what remains uncertain, and
  the person authorises under stated uncertainty.
- **A waiver per gate (Argon) or per stop (neither said).** The call here
  is per stop: a stop is what a person is standing at, it already has a
  kind and a reason, and a catalogue of gate names becomes the next wall.
- **`close --force`.** Argon: deprecate it. Not taken now: it is the way
  to abandon a plan, and its help is made to say so.
- **Defect reports from consumer repositories.** Both wanted a bundle a
  person previews and sends. Owed, as its own session.

## What was wrong or overstated

- Argon said the blocked-stop example was cleared by "overwriting a router
  report". The report is the engine's own answer, not a file the router
  owns.
- Argon counted about 45 of the 72 stops as walls the Mechanic's licence
  forbids. The stops were not sorted into walls and ordinary pauses; most
  `verification` stops are a review doing its work and most `publish`
  stops are a release waiting. The counts say stops are frequent, not how
  many were unfair.
- Zero Mechanic events means it was never opened here, not that it tried
  and failed.

## What the operator added afterwards

The three roles are there to make what the person is told fair, balanced
and plain -- in most cases nobody is cheating and there is a real blocker.
Everything said about an exception must be where an AI consult can read
it. A rollback should not cost the work, and a consult recommending one
should know that: the rollback already keeps uncommitted work on a local
branch, and the stop's own sentence said the opposite.
