--- [2/2] What the numbers say ---

1. NO CEILING WAS FOUND UP TO 12 WORKERS ON THIS HOST. That is an
   observed LOWER BOUND, not the ceiling the session set out to find, and
   the difference matters: counts above 12 were never run, so nothing here
   says where the ceiling is. The 14-core host measured w=12 at 350.5s
   with 8 FAILURES; DENICI ran w=12 green in 189s.

   THE SESSION'S CENTRAL HYPOTHESIS REMAINS UNRESOLVED. It was that "the
   failures and the poor speedup have one cause, and it is shared state,
   not CPU." Testing it requires re-running the previously-failing
   8-worker configuration ON THE HOST WHERE IT FAILED. That was not done,
   and could not be: the 14-core work machine is a different physical
   machine, not available from this session. Two variables therefore
   differ between the failing runs and these -- the isolation change AND
   the host -- so this sweep cannot attribute the difference to either
   one. It is a host-specific observation of DENICI through 12 workers and
   nothing more.

   OWED RESIDUAL -- OWNER: SESSION 2, AS A PRECONDITION.
   A post-isolation re-run of w=8 (and an upward sweep) on the 14-core
   host. Verification raised this twice: once in round 1 and again in
   round 2, where the fix was REJECTED on the correct ground that
   documenting a missing measurement does not perform it. The operator
   adjudicated on 2026-08-10 (see decisions.jsonl): the finding is
   accepted as true and unmet, not waived as wrong, and its discharge
   transfers to Session 2 -- the session where a worker count is actually
   adopted, which is the decision the measurement protects.

   SESSION 2 MUST NOT adopt a worker count for the 14-core host until it
   has run this, and must not read DENICI's clean result through 12
   workers as permission to raise the smaller host. If Session 2 also
   runs on DENICI, the residual carries forward to Session 3 unchanged
   and must be restated, not quietly dropped.

2. SPEEDUP SATURATES AT ROUGHLY 4 WORKERS. Marginal wall clock bought by
   each additional worker:

     1 -> 4    -317s over 3 workers   = 105.7s per worker
     4 -> 8     -25s over 4 workers   =   6.3s per worker
     8 -> 12    -17s over 4 workers   =   4.3s per worker

   Tripling the workers from 4 to 12 buys 1.22x. The knee is at 4 and it
   is not close.

3. THE COST IS PER-TEST INFLATION, AND IT IS LARGE. At 12 workers the
   machine does 2.46x the total work of a serial run to deliver 2.90x the
   wall-clock. Each Electron launch is competing with eleven others for
   CPU, disk and memory, so every test gets slower -- the slowest test
   goes 23.6s -> 84.0s, a 3.6x stretch, purely from load. This is the
   quantified version of the operator's complaint about `-n auto`: past
   the knee you spend the machine to buy seconds.

4. THE FLOOR IS THE SLOWEST TEST, AND IT MOVES. vsix-first-run-walkthrough
   (a real venv + pip install driven through the VS Code UI) is the
   longest test at every count up to 8: 23.6s serial, 31.4s at w=4, 47.0s
   at w=8, 66s at w=12. Above 8, icon-render-mechanism overtakes it at
   84.0s. No worker count can finish faster than whichever test is
   currently longest, which is why w=12's 189s is close to the practical
   floor for this suite as written.

--- [3/3] The race-vs-contention verdict (spec step 4) ---

Spec step 4 asks for this either way: if failures persist under full
isolation they are product races exposed by a schedule this suite has
never run, and they must be written down rather than relabelled as
flakiness. Failures did persist. Here is what they actually are.

EVERY FAILURE OBSERVED THIS SESSION, IN 12 RUNS, IS ONE TEST:
vsix-first-run-walkthrough. The other 34 tests did not fail once, at any
worker count, in any run. (34, not 35: real-host-baseline was excluded
from every run, so this session says nothing about it.)

READ THE SAMPLE SIZES BEFORE THE PATTERN. Every cell below is 1 to 4
runs. That is enough to establish that failures HAPPEN and where, and it
is not enough to establish why, or to compare rates between rows.

  condition                       runs   failures
  -----------------------------   ----   --------
  w=1  full suite                    1      0
  w=4  full suite                    1      0
  w=8  full suite                    4      2   <- both vsix-first-run
  w=12 full suite                    3      0
  vsix-first-run ALONE, serial       3      1   <- same test

  (The four w=8 runs are: one before HOME/USERPROFILE scoping landed and
  three after. Restricted to full isolation it is 3 runs, 1 failure.)

TWO DISTINCT FAILURE MODES. They are recorded SEPARATELY and are not
pooled, because they fail at different places for different reasons and
neither one is evidence about the other.

  Mode 1 -- spec line 208, 2 occurrences, BOTH at w=8:
    F1 pressed, `.quick-input-widget input` never visible within 15s.
    Occurred once before HOME/USERPROFILE scoping and once after, i.e.
    ONCE IN THE THREE FULLY-ISOLATED w=8 RUNS. Never observed at w=1,
    w=4 or w=12 -- but see the sample sizes below before reading anything
    into that.

  Mode 2 -- spec line 199, 1 occurrence, running ALONE at w=1:
    the tree never reached "2 sets", stuck on "Default0 sets" for the
    full 300s. That wait is gated on a REAL `pip install` completing
    inside the launched window.

CAUSE: UNRESOLVED FOR BOTH MODES. The samples are too small to support
any causal claim, and an earlier draft of this file made three that the
evidence does not carry. They are withdrawn:

  - "NOT CPU contention" -- withdrawn. It rested on 0 failures in 3 runs
    at w=12. At a per-run failure rate anywhere near the w=8 observation,
    3 clean runs is an unremarkable outcome, not a refutation. A higher
    worker count also does not guarantee that this particular test's F1
    keypress met heavier instantaneous load.
  - "NOT shared state" -- withdrawn. One failure in three fully-isolated
    w=8 runs is consistent with the isolation having reduced the rate, or
    not changed it, or the failure having another cause entirely.
  - "Mode 2 is network-bound" -- withdrawn. Nothing was captured proving
    PyPI was slow. Pip configuration (index URL, proxy, certificates,
    credentials) normally lives under HOME or APPDATA, BOTH of which this
    change now scopes to an empty directory -- so the isolation itself is
    a live candidate cause of Mode 2, not an exonerated bystander. No pip
    output was retained to distinguish them.

WHAT THE SPEC REQUIRES OF MODE 1, and it is the operative reading: step 4
says failures that persist under full isolation "are product races
exposed by a schedule this suite has never run -- a real finding, out of
scope to fix, and it must be written down rather than relabelled as
flakiness." Mode 1 did persist under full isolation, at w=8. It is
therefore recorded here as an OPEN PRODUCT-RACE CANDIDATE, not as
flakiness. Calling it flakiness is precisely what the spec forbids, and
an earlier draft of this file did it.

Per the spec's non-goals ("No fixing of product races... Recording them
precisely is in scope; repairing them is a later set"), neither mode is
fixed here.

CONSEQUENCE FOR SESSION 2, and it is blocking: Session 2's control is
"three consecutive green runs at the chosen setting before it is
trusted." This test failed in 2 of 9 full-suite runs across all worker
counts, so three consecutive green runs is a materially weaker signal
than it looks. Session 2 must decide explicitly how to handle it -- and
should NOT simply discount the failures as unrelated flake, because Mode
1's cause is open and may be exactly the schedule sensitivity the control
exists to detect. Note also that spec decision 4's diagnostic ("a failure
that passes in isolation is a race candidate") does not discriminate
here: this test has failed BOTH in parallel and in isolation, in
different places.

CONSEQUENCE FOR THE CEILING: the other 34 tests were clean in every run
at every count (1, 4, 8, 12) across 9 full-suite runs. No correctness
bound was reached on this host up to 12 workers. What bounds the setting
in practice is diminishing returns -- the knee at 4 -- and the operator's
desktop, not observed failure.

--- Not measured, and why ---

  - Counts between 12 and 20 were not swept. The operator's standing
    directive (2026-08-10) caps parallelism on this machine at 8 because
    unbounded parallelism starves the desktop; 12 was probed only to
    locate the ceiling and is recorded as ceiling data, NOT as an
    adoptable setting. Sweeping to 20 would have cost machine time to
    measure a configuration nobody may use.
  - real-host-baseline was excluded from every run above. Its own
    re-baselining is Session 2's job, and commit 5388c3d1's caveat
    applies: a cold launch against a FRESH APPDATA is not the same
    measurement as one against a warm shared profile, so its 1.9-min
    figure must be re-measured after this isolation rather than compared
    across it.
  - PIP CONFIGURATION UNDER THE SCOPED DIRECTORIES was not validated, and
    it is a real exposure rather than a theoretical one. A user's pip
    index URL, proxy, certificate bundle and credentials normally live
    under HOME or APPDATA (`%APPDATA%\pip\pip.ini`, `~/.config/pip`),
    and both are now empty per launch -- so a machine that depends on a
    corporate pip index would see the walkthrough's real `pip install`
    behave differently under the harness than by hand. The APPDATA half of
    that exposure shipped in 5388c3d1, before this session. It is named
    here as an open risk and as a live candidate cause of the Mode 2
    failure above; it is not resolved, and the pip environment the
    walkthrough needs is not documented anywhere.
  - The Python harness subprocess environment (electronLaunch.ts
    `_filteredEnv`) was deliberately NOT given a scoped HOME. It creates
    the git fixtures, and an empty HOME there risks losing git's global
    identity during fixture setup -- a different risk from the launched
    window, which the sample-project scaffold already handles with
    `git config --local`. Named here as a deliberate non-change, not an
    oversight.
